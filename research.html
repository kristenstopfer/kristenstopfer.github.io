Research

Kristen is a trainee in the field of Health Psychology. She is currently a Master's candidate in Human Development and Family Sciences at Texas Tech University, writing her thesis titled "Early Life Indicators of CVD Risk: Perceived Stress, Blood Pressure, and Blood Pressure Variability Across the Life Course in the NHLBI Growth and Health Study." She works in the Stress, Aging, and Relationships (STAR) Lab under the advisement of Dr. Ryan Linn Brown, where she is interested in the cardiovascular correlates of early widowhood. As a member of the STAR Lab, Kristen is building out the dyadic psychophysiology data collection space and assists in the development of two studies, one of which is an EMA study aimed at better understanding physiological changes in early widowhood, and the other which is centered on physiological synchrony in spousal caregivers. In addition to her work in the STAR lab, Kristen volunteers in the Parenting and Early Development (PEDs) Lab, where she helps to train undergraduates in psychophysiology (electrocardiography (ECG), impedance cardiography (ICG), and electrodermal activity (EDA)). She also volunteers in the Child, Adolescent, Resilience, and Ecology (CARE) Lab, where she is gaining exposure to mixed-methods study design in the context of family dynamics.

Kristen did her undergraduate studies at the University of Pittsburgh, where she first developed her love of research. She worked in the Laboratory for Psychophysiology and Health Neuroscience (LaPHN) for 3 years under the advisement of Drs. Pete Gianaros and Mark Scudder. Here she gained expertise in psychophysiology data collection and scoring, including ECG, ICG, continuous BP, and venous occlusion plethysmography. During her time at LaPHN she trained 14 other undergraduates on these methods, coauthored 6 posters that were presented at national and international conferences, and coauthored 2 publications. She continues to collaborate with her colleagues at LaPHN, and is currently writing a first-author manuscript titled "Autonomic Correlates of Subclinical CVD in Midlife Adults" (preregistered here) using data from the Neurobiology of Adult Health (NOAH) Study, where she played a large role in data collection and scoring. Her love of psychophysiology led her to create and lead a cross-institutional journal club, which meets monthly on zoom to discuss studies on psychophysiology across a variety of contexts.

Kristen is early on in her career, so her research interests are vast! She has expertise in cardiovascular psychophysiology, and pictured here is Kristen presenting her project on "Autonomic Correlates of Subclinical CVD in Midlife Adults" at the Society for Biopsychosocial Science and Medicine's 83rd Meeting in Chicago. She is particularly interested in blood pressure and blood pressure variability (BPV) across different time scales, which has led her to examine long-term BPV in her Master's thesis. In future work she is open to exploring other physiological systems, including but not limited to neuroscience, immunology, and endocrinology. Beyond physiology, Kristen is interested in both objective and subjective stress, including bereavement, loneliness, social isolation, acute psychological stress, neighborhood deprivation, socioeconomic status, and more. Ultimately, Kristen seeks to study how our experiences and perceptions of stress and our social worlds shape health across the lifecourse. She hopes to further incorporate the adverse health consequences of the ongoing climate crisis into her future work (again, her interests are vast!).

In addition to her studies, Kristen is highly involved in professional development through her service activities, particularly in the Society for Biopsychosocial Science and Medicine (SBSM). She currently serves as the Chair of the Climate, Sustainability, and Health Special Interest Group (SIG), where she works alongside fellow SIG members to promote implementation of sustainable practices and engagement with the climate crisis at both the indivudal member level and the society level. She is additionally on the Resources subcommittee within the Emerging Leaders SIG, and she recently assisted in the planning and execution of a virtual Mentor-Mentee event which connected SBSM trainees with mentors that aligned with their research interests and professional development goals.
